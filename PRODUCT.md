## Vision — through Bagrut, June 2027

**Phase A (now → 2027-03-31): a lifestyle business.** Two tutors, full
calendars, and the software does the prep. This is not a growth phase — it
is the point where the product earns its keep by giving Nikol her time
back.

**Phase B trigger, not a date.** Phase B — the brand growing beyond two
tutors — starts only when all three are true at once: both calendars are
full, prep time is at 10 minutes or less per lesson, and families are
waiting for a slot. Until all three hold, this document says nothing
further about phase B; anything more would be a guess.

**North stars, ranked:**
1. Nikol's prep time. A feature that adds a manual step for her is a net
   loss, even if the feature itself is good.
2. A parent sees evidence of progress without having to ask for it.

**Targets for 2027-03-31:**

| Target | Measured by |
|---|---|
| 10 active students | ≥1 completed lesson in the last 30 days |
| Prep time from 60 to 10 min per lesson | strict autogenerated share, plus a monthly check-in with Nikol (no timer, no manual logging) |
| 80% of lessons autogenerated (a lesson's homework is generated with it) | Share of generation runs in the last 30 days (the `lessons` table). **Strict:** reached `ready` with no tutor edit or restore. **Lenient:** produced material at all (`ready` or `held`). Shown next to coverage: generation runs vs lessons taught |
| ₪10k a month, about ₪1,000 per family | `payments`; the mechanism is steering families to כפול (90 min a week) |

**Gate:** no new features outside the pillars below until Codex lesson
generation runs end-to-end in production for 10 real lessons in a row,
clean. This is a checkable replacement for the older "stabilize the
foundations first" instinct.

**Decision authority:** Lior owns the business and settles every decision —
teaching, design, and marketing included. Nothing here is parked as
"pending Nikol."

Measured by `scripts/vision-metrics.mjs` at the monthly check-in (how to
run: `server/README.md` → Vision metrics).

## Pillars

Priority order below is the order work gets picked up in; the reliability
floor sits underneath all five and is never traded against them.

### Lesson autopilot

Done for phase A: a generated plan, slides, and homework reach the
student's page without Nikol touching them, for at least 80% of lessons
(a lesson's homework is generated with it; the strict share). Homework
is generated from what was actually taught in that lesson, not from the
plan in the abstract, and Nikol can still edit when she has to — editing
is a release valve, not the default path.

Todoist: label `pillar-autopilot`

### Progress parents can see

Done for phase A: a parent's page shows what was covered and what the
child understood, drawn from lesson reports and practice evidence (game
results tied to plan skills) — without the parent having to message the
tutor to ask.

Todoist: label `pillar-progress`

### Word of mouth

Done for phase A: every booking records where the family heard of מאה
בקליק, so word of mouth becomes a number instead of an impression, and a
parent has a way to share their child's progress with someone else.

Todoist: label `pillar-wom`

### Ninety minutes by default

Done for phase A: booking recommends כפול (90 minutes) as the default
plan, and families average about 90 minutes a week — more lesson time per
family without adding a booking step.

Todoist: label `pillar-90min`

### Ready for a third tutor

Done for phase A: no tutor name is hardcoded anywhere in the codebase — a
third tutor could be added as data (a row), not a code change. This is the
precondition for phase B, not phase B itself.

Todoist: label `pillar-tutor3`

### Floor: reliability

Not a pillar to advance — a floor the other five stand on. Done means:
backups actually restore (not just run), deploys pin a specific commit
rather than a moving tag, and a calendar or mail failure reaches the tutor
instead of failing silently.

Todoist: label `@Infra`

## Non-goals (this horizon)

- Automatic payment collection.
- A marketplace, or tutor choice at booking.
- Subjects other than math.
- A native app or PWA.
- The WhatsApp API for families.
- Selling the platform to other tutors.

## Binding decisions

- **Pricing:** יחיד ₪120 / כפול ₪215 / משולש ₪300. Single source of truth
  in code: `src/lib/plans.ts` — the booking form, confirmation email, and
  success summary all render from it. Change it there, not on individual
  pages.
- **Codex is the lesson engine**, and only Codex — not Claude or the
  Anthropic API. Authorization is a local file (`$CODEX_HOME/auth.json`,
  written by `codex login`), not a cloud API key. Any engine-dependent
  planning should start from that fact.
- **No PIN, no family password.** Identity travels in signed links: an
  `account` link reaches the whole family, a `student` link reaches one
  child's board, and a short join code is the fallback for a device with
  no WhatsApp. This was a deliberate decision, not a temporary compromise.
- **Payments are recorded by hand.** The product keeps a ledger; it does
  not collect money. Automatic collection (Bit/PayBox) was evaluated and
  explicitly rejected on 2026-08-23.
- **Two named tutors, shown by face.** The landing page sells Nikol and
  Lior by name, with testimonials that name them individually. This is
  the positioning, not decoration.
- **The repo is private.** Flipped from public to private on 2026-08-22
  after a routine check found it already held real student PII (names, a
  phone number, homework files, payment amounts) committed in plain
  Markdown and PDFs.
- **Student and payment PII never goes in this repo.** See
  `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-18-student-records-out-of-the-repo-design.md`
  for where that data actually lives and how the app reaches it.

## Users

**Parent (primary buyer).** A Hebrew-speaking Israeli parent, usually on a phone,
often mid-errand. Their job: get a specific problem their child is stuck on
solved this week, then see that it is actually being solved. They book, they
pay by hand (Bit/transfer, tracked manually), and they want evidence of progress
without having to ask for it.

**Student.** A school-age child, typically כיתה ז–יב, on their own phone or a
shared family device. Their job: find out what homework is due and do it — which
in this product means playing the practice game their tutor assigned, not reading
a worksheet. They rarely have an email address of their own.

**Adult self-learner.** Some students are old enough to be their own account
holder: they book, they pay, and there is no parent in the loop. The data model
already anticipates this (`accounts.is_self`). This is not an edge case to
bolt on later — it is a first-class shape of the same account.

**Tutors: Nikol and Lior — the two tutors the site sells by name, so "both
calendars" means theirs. Lior is also the owner, builds the platform, and
makes every product decision.** Day to day, Nikol does the teaching,
design review, and advertising content; Lior owns the business, builds and
maintains the site and backend, and makes every decision — including on
Nikol's areas (see Decision authority under Vision). Both read this repo.

## Positioning

Two named, visible tutors — not a marketplace, not an agency roster. The
landing page sells them by name and face, with real testimonials naming them.

The mechanism a neighboring tutoring site could not truthfully copy: lesson
materials and practice games are generated per student per lesson and land in
that student's own portal as playable homework. Twelve game templates run from
a shared engine against per-lesson JSON.

## Product Principles

1. **The family must never depend on the tutor to get in.** Any flow whose
   completion requires Nikol to send something by hand fails the north star.
2. **Nothing the user must remember.** The product's own history is a password
   invented once, never returned, and demanded by three later screens. Identity
   travels in links the product sends, not in the user's memory.
3. **One account, many students, one of whom may be the account holder.** The
   parent/child split is a property of the account, not two separate products.
4. **Records store facts; URLs, prices, and labels are derived.** Already true
   of game URLs and prices; it should stay true of anything new in the same
   family.
5. **Fail loudly to the tutor, gracefully to the family.** A calendar write or a
   generation job may fail; the booking must still complete, and the tutor must
   still find out.

## Terminology

- **קישור אישי** (personal/magic link) — how identity travels now that there
  is no password. An `account` link reaches the whole family; a `student`
  link reaches one child's own board.
- **קוד צירוף** (join code) — a short, one-time fallback a parent hands to a
  child's device that has no WhatsApp, when a link can't be sent directly.
- **חשבון** — the account holder: a parent, or a self-paying adult learner.
- **תלמיד/ה** — a student under an account. A family is one חשבון with one
  or more תלמידים.

One noun each, everywhere — these replace the older, drifting mix of
סיסמה / קוד / פין, which no longer exists in the product.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **מאה בקליק** ("100 in a click"). Wordmark and logo assets in
  `static/images/logos/`.
- The two tutors are named and shown by face on the landing page. Testimonials
  name them individually. This is the positioning, not decoration.
- Hebrew voice is warm, direct, and parent-facing — `נחזור אליכם`, `הקלידו`,
  second person plural. Not corporate, not childish.
- Pricing ladder is named in Hebrew idiom: **יחיד / כפול / משולש**. Those names
  are the product's own vocabulary and the booking flow should use them rather
  than inventing "45 / 90 / 135 דקות" as the primary label.
- Do not fabricate volume, ratings, or customer counts anywhere in the
  product. This is an early business; real numbers live in
  `scripts/vision-metrics.mjs`'s output, not in copy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **RTL Hebrew is the primary and only locale.** Directionality is a
  correctness requirement, not a style preference: `direction: ltr` on slot
  rows and `text-transform: uppercase` on Hebrew day labels are bugs.
- Phone-first. The parent decides on a phone; touch targets, thumb reach, and
  input font size (≥16px, or iOS Safari zooms the viewport) are functional
  requirements.
- Contrast must clear WCAG AA 4.5:1. Enforced by
  `tests/unit/tokens-contrast.test.mjs` against the tokens in
  `src/lib/styles/tokens.css` — any accent that can't clear AA as text is
  restricted to fills only, with a separate, darker token for text, borders,
  and focus rings.
- Children are users. Copy on the student board is read by a 13-year-old.

## System in brief

- **Booking** — a 3-step wizard: plan (duration/name/price from
  `src/lib/plans.ts`), a real slot read from the calendar, and contact
  details including an email (used to find or create the family's account).
- **Dashboard** — the tutor's view: upcoming lessons, balance owed, student
  cards, lessons awaiting a report, and unresolved calendar-write failures.
- **Family portal** — passwordless entry (`/enter`) via a signed link;
  parent and student views, each scoped to what that identity may see.
- **Games** — twelve interactive templates run by one engine against
  per-lesson JSON, reached through a signed URL.
- **Lesson generation** — Codex produces a plan, slides, and homework per
  booked lesson, fire-and-forget so it never blocks the booking response.
- **Learning plans & reports** — a per-enrollment skill tree, updated by an
  append-only event log; a one-line end-of-lesson report from the tutor.
- **Payments ledger** — integer-agorot records, marked paid by hand from
  the dashboard; no automatic collection.
- **Calendar** — two independent paths: an iCal feed for reading
  availability, a Google service account for writing booked events.
- **Email + cron** — booking and portal-link mail via Gmail/nodemailer;
  scheduled reminders run as VPS `systemd --user` timers.

**Data model.** The entity model (`accounts`, `students_v2`, `teachers`,
`enrollments`, `bookings_v2`, `payments`, `homework`, `results_v2`,
`join_codes`, plus the learning-plan and report tables) is authoritative
and wired into every runtime path listed above. `lessons` is the live
lesson-generation table; its intended successor, `lessons_v2`, exists but
is unadopted — nothing in production writes it, and reading it is
forbidden because it silently returns an empty result. `students` and
`bookings` are draining: still read so old rows stay visible, never
written to again. `results` still receives game plays that cannot be
attributed to a student id (`src/routes/api/game-result/+server.ts`).
`calendar_failures` is the live table for failed calendar writes
(`flagCalendarFailure` in `db.ts`); `calendar_failures_v2` is dead. Full
status of every table is in `src/lib/server/storage-inventory.ts`.

**Channels.** Families are reached by email, or by a `wa.me` link the
parent opens on their own device — there is no WhatsApp Business API
integration. CallMeBot delivers WhatsApp messages to one pre-registered
number only, the tutor's; it cannot message a customer.

**Hosting.** One Node process (SvelteKit, `adapter-node`) serves the site
and the API together, in a Docker container behind Caddy on a
self-hosted VPS, with SQLite under `DATA_DIR`. CI builds and tags each
image by commit SHA; the VPS pulls a pre-built image rather than building
from source, and a deploy follows every merge to `main`.

See `server/README.md` and `src/lib/server/storage-inventory.ts` for
detail beyond this summary.
